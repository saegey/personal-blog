Pre-Race & Setup
	1.	How did you prepare for the high-altitude start (nearly 8k feet) — anything different in sleep, hydration, or pacing strategy?
	  - We got there two days before and pre rode some of the trails the day before and were noticing the altitude. My wife was affected more but i could notice it. We didn't have much time to get here early so not able to full acclimate.  For hydration this was a tough one. Looking back I would have used a hydration pack but I had three bottles on my bike and never touchd the third. The first half of the course was so gnarly that it was hard to drink and fuel. Luckily the stage was shorter so it wasn't a big deal. I didn't yet own a hydration pack at this point so it wasn't an option. Also the fast pros, had hydration packs.
	2.	What gear choices did you make (tires, gearing, layers) knowing you’d start cold and at elevation?
		 - I went with 650b wheels and WTB sendero 47c knobby tires because of the 25 miles of single track. I also though it would help a bit with gearing since I had a 2x 50-34 11/34. I had to manage the location of the chain with all the rock gardens to make sure it didn't bounce of. I did have a couple hard hits on the rims but no flats.

Early Race Dynamics
	3.	How did the race roll out from Galena Lodge — was it a hard start, a controlled group, or immediate separation?
	    - it was a neutral rollout and I got a really good position up front but shortly after a gravel climb when things fell apart I followed a guy on a wrong trail and didn't realize it till 5 min later and this really hurt a lot. I back tracked but was stuck behind a tons of slow traffic especially in uphill of a flow trail and a lot of people were walking. It was miserable but I was able to pass a lot of people on the single track and get back to it but it costs me about 20 minutes of time in the end I think.
	4.	Did you notice the altitude impacting your breathing or power early on compared to lowland races?
	    - I felt great that day and didn't really start to slow down to the very end of the race. The second half of the course was a slog with two way traffic and some blind corners. I just tried to stay safe and focus and push a good pace.

Mid-Race Flow
	5.	How did you handle nutrition and hydration at altitude — did your stomach tolerate it, or did you need to adjust timing/amounts?
	    - Hydration and nutrition were a bit of a challenge. I had three bottles but only used two. I was able to eat a lot of food and didn't have any issues with my stomach. I had Roctane in the bottles so I could get some calories in. I also had a couple gels and some chews. There were no aid stations so I had to be self sufficient.
	6.	Were there any decisive climbs, descents, or technical sections mid-stage where groups split or tactics mattered?
		 - Not really just to get a good position on the single track. 

Key Moments
	7.	What was the most memorable or defining section of this stage — a brutal climb, a fast gravel descent, or a moment of group cooperation (or lack thereof)?
		- some really fun decents and amazing views of the surrounding mountains. The Merriman trail also had some amazing views of the mountains and the river. Also the river crossing was fun and a bit sketchy. I wasn't going to even attempt to ride through that.
	8.	Did you have any mechanical issues, crashes, or close calls that shaped your day?
	    - Not any mechanical issues. Just the wrong turn at the beginning. Kind of a bummer because i could have finished well without it.

Endgame & Finish
	9.	How did you feel rolling into the finish — still strong, or holding on after a tough day at altitude?
	    - I felt good but was tired. I had to burn a lot of extra matches trying to gain time since I knew I was far behind 20 min into the stage with the wrong turn. 
	10.	Looking back, what went especially well in Stage 1, and what do you wish you’d done differently?
	    - the gear was good and my tech skills were very good. I would bring a hydration pack and do better listening to my garmin. There may be a better setup that alerts I am way off course. I was just following the guy in front of me and didn't realize it was wrong until I was way off course. I should have been more aware of the course and the map.
