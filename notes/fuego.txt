		Pre-Race & Setup
	1.	You put a lot of effort into dialing your bike — new damper, electronic shifting, dropper post, tires with inserts, bar adjustments. Which upgrade do you feel made the biggest difference on race day, and why?
	   - it all made a difference but the electronic shifting was probably the biggest upgrade. also the lockout. Another thing was dealing with was some brake pads glazing over on the shimano dual piston brakes. I had to sand them down a bit and that didn't fix it so i replaced both sets to be safe since I had invested so much into this bike and event. I could feel like I skipped out or was lazy about dialing my equipment.
	2.	You mentioned riding Marin and Santa Cruz trails before Sea Otter. How did those trail sessions shape your confidence going into the Fuego XL compared to your Pacific Northwest training?
	    - They helped and getting a bunch of time on the mountain bike also helped. That was the only bike I brought on the 2 week road trip so I was riding it pretty much every day and tweaking things with position and such to make sure it was dialed come race day. The trails in Fort Ord were much sandier than I was used to (more similar to Bend where are raced the year prior) but because of some floods they were rutted out and just pretty unforgiving. I wonder if a full-suspension would that help traction. I was pushing my limits on the descents and still slower then a lot of the competition around me. I would be able to close the gaps on the climbs though.
	3.	How was the atmosphere at Laguna Seca leading up to the race? Did the expo, crowds, or pre-ride energy add to the anticipation?
	    - It was very cool. I had heard about it and seen coverage of it but it felt special to be there in person and see all the pro up close and personal. We also ran into a friend of ours Gabe at his Carbs Fuel booth in the infield. We walked around to alot of the booths and hung out the day before and did a little pre-riding and saw some people. Parking and getting intl Laguana seca and driving from Seacliff where we were stay was a bit of a pain but it was worth it. 

Early Race Chaos
	4.	The Mazda Raceway climb is a decisive moment. What was the scene like at the top — were you able to hold position, or did you have to burn a big match to stay in contact?
	   - A lot of people diving into a right turn and just trying to hold position and then straight into some steep sketchy sanding descending. We pre rode this the day before and I felt good about it, but on race day it was a different story. The crowded field also affected visibility so it was hyper sensory overload trying to steeer through all of it and stay upright. The entire race I had zero crashes which is awesome but plenty of close calls that I narrowly avoided..
	5.	Those early descents sound hectic. Was there a particular moment where you felt at risk of crashing or boxed in? How did you manage that chaos?
	    - Yeah I was really trying to stay safe and not get caught in anything. This is a long race and I knew I had to conserve energy for later. I focused on smooth lines and letting faster riders go by instead of forcing my way through. Also, a lot of people take risks and a lot dont finish the race. I was wanting to do well but finish in one piece with no issues. Also, I had to remember this is a premier mountain bike event and the competition here is different than your local mountain bike race. A lot these people having been doing this for decades.

Middle of the Race
	6.	At the 41k and 54k splits, you were holding ~53rd/52nd in your age group. Do you remember how you felt in that middle section — were you still racing aggressively, or settling into survival mode?
	   - yeah i was aggressive the whole time, but past the midway point my average power was dropping off 20-30 watts from what it was on the first lap. I was still pushing hard but I was also trying to be smart about it. I was still passing people and but also getting passed. I was also trying to stay with a group of riders that were around my level and not get dropped. 
	7.	You carried an Osprey pack plus bottles. How did the weight and convenience of that setup feel over 100k — would you do it the same way again?
	   - yeah i would do it again. It crazy to be riding for 6 hours and not put a foot down the whole time or maybe i did but never got off the bike.

Finale & Finish
	8.	At 74k you were up to 29th, but slipped to 37th by the finish. Do you recall what changed in those final 20k — fatigue, technical mistakes, group dynamics?
	   - probably just fatigue and but my times up Lookout ridge only differ by 10 seconds so maybe i was cooked at the first lap. The normalized power is 199wats for 6hrs which aint too bad in my eyes.
	9.	What emotions did you have crossing the line? Relief, pride, frustration? How did it compare to your expectations when you started?
	    - It felt good to be finished and accomplished. I had put in a lot of hard work leading up to this race, and while I didn't achieve all my goals, I was proud of my effort and the progress I've made. It took a lot to get to this point, and I felt like I had raced smart and stayed safe throughout the day. I was also happy to have finished in one piece with no crashes or major issues, which is always a win in my book.

Analysis & Takeaways
	10.	You said more MTB time and faster descending would help. Were there specific sections of the course where you lost ground on descents or technical features?
	  -  Yes, I definitely felt like I lost time on some of the more technical descents. The sandy conditions made it tricky to maintain speed and control, and I noticed some riders were able to navigate those sections much more smoothly than I could. I think having more practice on similar terrain would help me find better lines and carry more speed through those areas. Also maybe the hard tail made it harder for me. I didn't see many out there. Also, I would like to do this race on a full suspension bike. I think it would help with traction and control on the descents, especially in the sandy conditions we had this year.
	11.	You targeted ~80g carbs/hr and felt good. Did you ever feel close to bonking or under-fueled, or was this the best nutrition execution you’ve had yet?
	 - no not at all. I felt like I had a good plan going in and executed it well. I was able to stay on top of my nutrition and hydration
	12.	Looking forward, how does this race shape your confidence for the rest of the season compared to Heaven of the South and your other early-year prep races?
    - This is my second long mountain bike race and I know what to expect and areas I can improve. They aren't easy and make gravel racing feel more approachable. But I like the challenge and the variety it brings to my racing. I feel like I have a solid foundation now, and I can build on that for the rest of the season. I also think that the skills and endurance I've developed from these races will carry over and help me perform better in future events.
