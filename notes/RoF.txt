Race Context & Prep
	1.	How did this race fit into your season plan? Was it a goal event or a spontaneous addition?
	    - It was actually a transfer from my original goal of the High Cascades 100 miles mountain bike race. It was on the same and similar trails but 50ish  miles in length. I was pretty burned out from training and also had COVID-19 in the recently, so I was not in the best shape and it turned out to be a good decision. It tends to be a heat wave in that area in July and it was nice to have a shorter race to focus on and more time to prepare.
	2.	What drew you to the Ring of Fire 50? Location, course reputation, training benefits?
	   - My goal for the year was some long mountain bike events and the Ring of Fire 50 offered a great opportunity to test my fitness and skills on a challenging course without the full commitment of a 100-mile race. The trails are faster and less technical than my local trails outside Seattle. We decided to combine the trip with a vacation and had the race on day 2 and the remaining 5 days to enjoy our time on the bike in outside Bend in Sun River.
	3.	How was your lead-up — training, tapering, fatigue levels, and confidence heading in?
	    - I was doing regular training but my numbers where below my peak levels for sure. I had done some mountain biking but not enough leading up to it that I would like but I cramed a few rides before on the bike that help boost my confidence. Earlier this year when training I did at 10k ft climbing and 85 miles with a friend to prep for these long races and I survived but with no wrist issues (nerve) from the straight bars. I was relatively rested going into it but had done a lot of training and racing earlier in the year (BWR Vancouver Island, Ephrata, a couple XC races) 

Course & Conditions
	4.	How would you describe the terrain and technical features? Flow, climbs, descents, singletrack vs. fire roads?
	    - Lots of climbing for the first 15 miles (1500 ft) put pretty flowing with some decents mixed in which was nice to have to some time to recover. Everything was full gas though. If you let up for any short period, someone would appear behind and mentally you dont want to be letting people pass all day so you push harder.
	5.	What were the weather conditions like at Mt. Bachelor that morning?
	    - extremely smoking, 150-160 aqi and the race was up in the air but they decided to go ahead with it. The smoke made it harder for sure and and also the the 5400ft starting altitude getting up to 7k at the high point. My heart rate was pegged high the entire race 157 average for 5 hours.  It was cold in the morning but heated up during the day.
	6.	Any standout features — volcanic dirt, dusty descents, rock gardens, creek crossings?
	    - Lots of volcanic dust, dusty decents, sandy loose corners, random super technical rock gardens that sometimes was better to dab through since I had pre-ridden any of these trails and never ridden in Bend before the day. Lots of flowing fun trails and some table tops and stuff in one section. The trails were quite fun but I was just not totally used to the increase in speed on these from around 7-8 avg here to 10.8 so reaction time needed to be quicker. 5 hours of this will tired you out and have you making mistakes if your not prepared.

Race Strategy & Execution
	7.	What was your pacing strategy going in? Did you aim for negative splits or just survive the climbs?
	    - Full gas as much as I could handle and rest on the decents. I felt strong but the 2nd half the climbs were a drag and felt i had lost some power.
	8.	How did you manage nutrition and hydration during the 50 miles?
	    - Osprey hydration with roctane and roctane in the bottles. Gels every hours and I filled up my hydration pack at half way point aid station with Roctane. The pack makes it a lot easier to drink and eat in this fast trails.
	9.	Did you ride solo most of the day or work with groups?
	    - With some different people throughout the day but nobody for any real extended period of time. There was one guy actually that i would catch up on the uphill and he was descending faster than me and we would yo yo some. I am faster than majority on the climbs and but as fast descending (especially on these fast sandy trails).
	10.	Any mechanicals, crashes, or other drama?
	    - So I had the rear slide out on some sands and scraped up my knew a tiny bit but it was really minor. I had some other close calls leading up to this. After that happened, I decided ride a little more conservative because a worse crash could end this race and I was already 36 miles into this race and not many places that I could really pick up at this point.  Later on maybe 45 miles in I had a rock push my front wheel and high sided on on my front and hit my throat and got my dirt and leaves in my mouth. This crash was a lot worse but I was still able to continue and at this point I was mostly angry and ready to be finished with this. My chain for the last 5 miles was sounding terrible from all the moon dust and we had to ride some deep double track sections of sand that really sucked. I finished and overall was pretty happey with the result. I could have done better but also did a lot of worse.

Performance & Results
	11.	How did the race unfold for you physically and mentally?
	    - Mentally trained, I think both crashes were really caused from the mental fatigue of riding for so long. I couldn't image doing 100 miles of this. Thats a whole anotehr level.
	12.	What was your final result or placement, and how do you feel about it?
	    - In the open men i was 28 out of 62 which is not too bad. I would have needed to gain 20 min to get to the top 20 which i think is doable if i had a really good day, zero crashes and maybe altitude acclimated. 
	13.	Anything from the stats file stand out — avg speed, elevation gain, normalized power, heart rate?
	    - High avg heart rate of 157, only like 5-7 min down time so pretty much moving 5 hrs strait. Power was strong for first 17 miles but dropped off some. Probably not the best endurance. I think was training but doing less long sessions at this point in the year.

Gear & Setup
	14.	What bike and setup did you run? (Suspension, tire choice, gearing)
	    - I had a Specialized chisel, xt deore 12 speed 10/51 and 32 front. Ground control  2.35, eliminator Grid 2Bliss Ready T5 2.35 x 29, Roval xc wheels, fox sid control fork 120mm, fox transfer post. The tires needed to be different. I originally bought this set for do-it-all aka downcountry but they were overkill here and I need sometime more file tread for all the sand and maybe somethign a little lighter and faster and more supple.
	15.	Did anything about your equipment make a noticeable difference on this course? 
        - the tires would have but I used what I had on the bike. I didn't too much tweaking for this. Just doing the training leading up to it was all I could handle at this period.

Takeaways & Reflections
	16.	Biggest highlights or moments where you felt strong?
	    - It was an accomplishment to finish despite two crashes because of the smoke and the struggle rescheduling to this race from High Cascades. It was also a relief to be finished and have a new long mountain bike race. Most of my mountain bike rides are under 20 miles. 5 hours full gas is big accomplishment.
	17.	Any low points or struggles you had to push through?
	    - After the second crash I was ready to be done but pushed through
	18.	Key lessons for next time or advice for someone racing Ring of Fire 50?
	    - Pre-ride the trails, more appropriate tires. Practice more. Better corning and speed control. Less braking aka free speed.
