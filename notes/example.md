## Before the Race
Coming into Stage 2 at Rebecca’s Private Idaho, the fatigue from the 50-mile mountain stage the day prior was still in my legs, but I felt surprisingly decent. This stage was different — a short but demanding gravel time trial: 4.6 miles, 1,392 feet of climbing, topping out at 8,500ft.  

Warm-up was tricky. The neutral rollout and staggered rider starts meant I was already cooled down by the time I reached the line. Legs felt good enough, though, and I made sure to gel up beforehand. I stuck with the same 700c Pathfinder Pro 42 tires from the previous day (switching off the 650B Sendero knobbies I’d used for the singletrack), not making any major gear changes.  

Fueling was simple: topped off before the effort. My only real question was how the altitude would hit me — I didn’t want to burn too hot and blow halfway up.  

## Race Narrative

### Neutral Rollout
The neutral rollout set a different tone than a normal TT. Honestly, it was kind of cool — I even got to ride alongside Payson McElveen for a bit. But the waiting around dulled the warm-up, and I had to reset mentally before my turn.  

### Early Climb
When the clock started, I went out hard but quickly found a strong, sustainable tempo. Pacing has always been a strength for me, and I leaned on feel rather than obsessing over the numbers.  

### Mid-Climb
The higher altitude was noticeable — not in a dramatic, gasping way, but in the subtle heaviness in my legs and lungs. Heat was less of an issue since the effort was short, and while smoke hung in the air, it didn’t noticeably drag on my performance. My focus stayed sharp on keeping momentum.  

### Key Moments
The gravel surface was cooperative: firm, predictable, and easy to hold a strong line. That meant I could focus entirely on watts-to-weight instead of traction or handling. Passing rider after rider gave me motivation to keep pressing. There wasn’t a single moment where I had to back off — instead, I steadily built pressure the whole way.  

### Finish
Crossing the line, I felt like I’d executed a clean ride. No big mistakes, no fading, no what-ifs. I slotted 26th out of 138, gaining back time on GC after losing some in Stage 1. The climb suited me, and it showed.  

## What Went Well
- **Pacing by feel**: Started strong but settled into an excellent tempo, never overreaching.  
- **Execution**: Clean, focused effort with no tactical or technical missteps.  
- **Mental boost**: Passing other riders gave momentum and confidence.  
- **Equipment**: Tires and setup were dialed for this type of gravel climb.  

## Needs Improvement
- **Warm-up logistics**: The neutral rollout and staggered wait left me a bit cooled down; better strategies for staying warm could help.  
- **Altitude adaptation**: Not a limiting factor here, but future high-altitude races may benefit from more acclimation.
