Pre-Race & Setup
	1.	How did you feel heading into BWR knowing it would be your longest ride ever — nervous, excited, or more curious to see if you could finish?
	 - I was mostly excited but also a bit nervous. I knew I could survive but not sure what pace or result would be like. I was also nervous of pinch points and stream crossings.
	2.	What gear/bike setup did you run (tires, gearing, hydration system), and how did you adapt it for BWR’s mix of road, singletrack, and rough sectors?
	 - I rode my santa cruz stigmata, shimano 12 speed di2 50/34 11/34 gearing with roval clx gravel wheels with Continental Terraspeed 35 which I thought would be fast on road but provide good traction on the sandier terrain in south california.
	3.	How did you fuel and hydrate during the event — did you use aid stations heavily, carry most of your own, or a mix? - I used a mix of aid stations and my own nutrition. I carried two 26 oz bottles and refilled at the aid stations. I also had a few gels and chews for quick energy but because of the length of the race and limited on bike storage I heavily relied on aid stations for food and hydration. The downside of this was the Hammer nutrition hydration mix containing stevia as a sweetener, which I really don't like at all. I prefer Skratch lemon lime. Looking back years later, my nutrition strategy was not ideal, and I would have been better off carrying more of my own food and hydration

Early Race Dynamics
	4.	How was the rollout — did you try to stay with a fast group, or settle in knowing the distance would be epic?
	   - the group was so large and we staged toward the back. We burned a lot of matches in the early road sections trying to move up before a up hill single track section we knew would be bottlenecked from previous years. Getting staged early and waiting is not that fun since you already are waking up early as it is but it is worth it if you want to finish well. Trying to advance with a group of 500 riders starting is tough. 
	5.	Were there any early climbs, sectors, or selections that defined the opening hours of your ride?
	   - The first decisive climb was 2.94 mile, 720ft climb culminating on single track at the end with lots of traffic. it took me 17:25 with 221 watt average.  There plenty of off-road sectors all through the course and it was always a relief to get back on the road. A lot of traffic in single track sections and never found a cohesive group to stay with. I remember seeing a mountain bike try to let a photographer know to take a photo of a rock garden section he was riding that everyone walked and he then went down really hard. We saw him later on so he must have not gotten to banged up.

Mid-Race Flow
	6.	How did your pacing strategy hold up over such a long course — did you ride steady, or go through big highs and lows?
	    - I tried to maintain a steady effort, but there were definitely moments of fatigue and mental lows, especially during the longer climbs.
	7.	Did you ride much in groups, pacelines, or mostly solo through the middle of the day?
	    - we rode in groups when we could, but the large number of riders made it hard to find a consistent group. I ended up riding solo with Ira for long stretches, especially on the more technical sections where it was hard to keep up with faster riders.
	8.	Any memorable trails, dirt sectors, or technical challenges that stood out mid-race?
	   - The sandy sections in the middle of the course were particularly challenging. I remember one stretch where I am already fatigued and just have grind a high power to push through it.  At around 60 miles is when the wheels starting coming off. It was an area called Black Canyon and it was a terrible washboard climb that was really hard to ride and rattled my helmet and brain. This was the moment I wished I had a fatter tire. Also I think at this point it was around 86 deg F and the heat became a factor for the rest of the day and the slow speeds on the climb compounded the heat. The descent was also gnarly and super technical with lots of loose rocks and sand. I remember thinking that this was the hardest. Also, my tool roll (silca with boa) came loose a few times due to all the bumps and I had to stop and fix it. My friend Ira got a head a lot in this sections but waited for me at the end of the descent. I was really grateful for that because I was pretty exhausted and it was nice to have someone to ride with again. Then we had a long sandy road section that was really hard to ride and we finally arrived to I think the second aid station. At this point I realized it was going to be a long day and I was not sure how I was going to finish. Finishing became a priority of racing since it was hot and the fatique was rolling in with still another 60 miles left to finish.

Key Moments
	9.	What was the hardest moment of the day for you — a specific climb, a mental low, or a physical challenge?
	- Having to finish the day out with the Double Peak climb which is so steep. The full climb was 3.66 miles 983ft at 5.1%. I average 191w for 26:00 for it. This is 128 miles into the ride so not too bad. There are ramps at the top that are avg 17% for .25 mile so it is a hard climb. They also had us riding on the sidewalk for some sections and having to hop these logs on the sidewalk. ugh.
	10.	Conversely, what was the most rewarding or beautiful part of the race?
	- the scenery was stunning, especially the views from the higher elevations. The combination of mountains, forests, and the winding course made for a beautiful backdrop. we went all over the place, farms, canyons, and more. Also, the traffic control and support from volunteers was fantastic, making the experience feel safe and well-organized. The camaraderie among riders also stood out, with many helping each other through tough sections. All the cheers from specators helped push you to continue on.

Endgame & Finish
	11.	How did the final miles play out — survival mode, a push for a time/place, or just soaking in the experience?
	   - survival mode for sure. I was exhausted and just focused on finishing. The last climbs were tough, but I tried to keep a steady pace and not push too hard. I was relieved to see the finish line and know that I had completed my longest ride ever. The descent coming down double peak when you know you are almost done is a great feeling. I was able to ride it pretty fast and it was a nice reward for all the hard work. I also had a lot of fun riding with Ira and we were able to finish together which was great.
	12.	What was your recovery like after such a massive effort, especially compared to your prior biggest ride (Snoqualmie double)?
	   - My recovery was definitely more challenging after this ride. The combination of distance, elevation, and heat took a toll on my body. I made sure to hydrate and refuel immediately after finishing, but I still felt the fatigue for several days. Compared to the Snoqualmie double, where I felt more prepared and had a better pacing strategy, this ride left me more drained. I learned the importance of recovery nutrition and rest, especially after such a long and demanding event.
