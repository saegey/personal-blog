Pre-Race & Setup
	1.	What was your mindset heading into the 2022 edition — early-season test, key target, or more of a tune-up?
	   - this was primarily a training race. It was the first race of the season and I had pretty strong fitness. I had never done the race before and I did some research. The gravel for the most part is pretty tame and it plays out more like game of attrition and more similiar to road races.
	2.	What bike and gear setup did you use (tires, gearing, clothing), and how did it compare to what you ran in 2023?
	    - I used my newly built Santa cruz stigmata with Bontrager 3V gravel carbon rims mounted to Specialed Roubaix 30/32 c tires. I wore Rapha pro team with jersey with vest and regular pro team bibs with winter level leg warmers. It was cold in the start and I was glad I had the vest. I also wore a cap under my helmet and gloves. I used 2x 50/34 11/34 cassette shimano di2 ultegra. Specialized mtb shoes and xt pedals. I think I had two - 24oz bottles onboard and some gels and nutrition.
	3.	What were conditions like at the start — cold, windy, dry, or any unique factors?
	    - cold at the start but clear skies. A bit windy in the middle half of the course.

Early Race Dynamics
	4.	How did the rollout and first gravel sectors play out — fast separation, or more gradual attrition?
	   - it is a neutral rollout from the school where the race starts. It lasts for about 15 min and is quite chaotic with a lot of jostling for position. It is important to get a close to the front because its a lot of effort to weave through the pack when the climb begings. For the first climb i averaged 312w for 12:48 on a climb that is 709ft over 2.49mi 5.4% gradient and 172bpm heart rate which is closer to my max of 182. the surface is quite loose in areas so you have to be careful not to spin out. The first climb is a bit of a bottleneck so it is important to be in a good position.
	5.	Were you able to stay with the lead group through the opening climbs, or did you find your rhythm in a chase group?
	   - Because of how big the rollout is I never really could see the front of the race. During the first two climbs that happend in the first 17 miles of the 87 miles race, it really determines your position and group for the remainder of the race. This suited me because I have good climbing power and for long steady efforts.  
	   - the second climb was 5.07 miles, 970 ft at 3.6%. I averaged 270w for 24:36 at a heart rate 169bpm. On this climb things had already broken up a lot and riders were yo-yoing all over the place. I think i pass a few and was really happy with my effort on the first two climbs.

Mid-Race Flow
	6.	How did the big climbs (like the Swakane Canyon climb or others, depending on the course that year) feel compared to expectations?
	 - After the 2nd climb is the Baird Springs descent. It was fast and fun, but I had to be careful not to push too hard and risk a crash. My tire choice made things a little sketchy. The descent was 15 miles long and lasted 40 min or so. I did everything I could to stay with the group I was in. We even went through a dark tunnel at the end and I forgot to pull my sunglasses off which was a bad move. After the tunnel, you hit a long road section where there is quite a bit of wind. The groups was probably 10-15 riders at this point. The next 25 miles are pretty smooth sailing minus the wind but all road and nothing technical or major climbs.
	7.	Did you ride in groups for much of the day, or spend a lot of time solo?
	    - Yes I rode as a group to the second aid station (actually wasn't supposed to be there and was added last minute). I stopped in with a friend Gabe that I had met at CX race the previous fall. We worked together to keep a steady pace and share pulls into the wind for the remained of the race. It was nice to stop for 3-4 minutes, stretch the back and take my gloves off.  If this aid station was not here, who knows if I would have been able to keep that pace.
	8.	How did your nutrition and hydration strategy hold up — any lessons from that year?
	   - So they have a bottle drop at aid station at 40 miles in but the group I was in blew right past it. I asked a rider if that was the aid we just passed and they commented, "Yeah, but this is only an 80 mile race, why would you need to stop?". haha i thought. I should have been more proactive about my nutrition and hydration, especially on such a long day but it never seemed to really catch up and bite me.

Key Moments
	9.	What was the hardest part of the course for you in 2022 — a climb, a descent, or the long flat sections?
	    - The first two climbs I really had to go all in for and make a hard effort. The descent pushed me to the limits as well because I couldnt see myself losing that group. The three devils climbs at miles 58-61 hurt quite a bit when the fatigue settles into the legs.
	10.	Did you have any memorable interactions with other riders, mechanical issues, or sketchy moments on gravel descents?
	    - So after the aid station. Gabe and I rode together the last 20 miles or so and shared the workload, taking turns at the front to shield each other from the wind. It was a great way to stay motivated and keep the pace up. We also chatted quite a bit about cycling and life and it passed the time and took away the feeling of pain in the legs. In the next years edition, this section would feel a lot worse and drag on for much longer.

Endgame & Finish
	11.	How did the final miles unfold — a group sprint, solo effort, or survival mode?
	    - Gabe and I ended up join two other riders in the last few miles and the sprinted for places which we didn't care to contest. at the end of the day, this was a training ride and I was mostly concerned with speed, time, normalized power and such for the ride and that all was good for this ride.
	12.	Looking back now (with the 2023 race as a comparison), what went especially well in 2022, and what would you improve if you could redo it?  - I think my pacing and group riding were solid, but I would focus more on nutrition and hydration strategy, especially with the added distance and difficulty of the course.
