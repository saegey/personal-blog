Pre-Ride & Arrival
	1.	How were your preparations leading into the March 10, 2024 ride?
(Think: sleep quality, gear decisions—tire widths, layers, hydration, nutrition—or anything logistical such as travel, arrival, and pre-ride feeling.)
	- I had ridden my first century of the year in January on the RCC WTR series and did 3 or 4 of those 80 miles+ rides.  My volume was solid for January, February and March leading up to this since I was motivated for Sea Otter Feugo XL that I had signed up for in December. Having a goal on the calendar early is helpful to stay focused on training and riding during the colder months of the year. We drove over a day before and pre-rode some of the course on Saturday including one of the longer climbs (Owens - Bateman to top 3.35mi 925ft 5.2%).  I rode this climb in the preride pretty hard (271w, 20:44, to test the legs. In the race it was 23:06, 291watt average )
	2.	In the opening miles (around Badger Canyon and McBee climbs), how did you feel physically and mentally? 
	  - I felt really strong on both those climbs. On the Badger climb, I average 296 watts (170bpm) for 18:59 for 3.29 mi 954 ft 5.5% grade. I nearly stayed with the front group but dropped back at the end because the pace was too strong. My friend Ben was with me at the stop. He stopped to pee and then chased back to the lead group. I was impressed.  Things exploded at the top of this climb and were strung out. I think the lead group was 6 or so and I was right behind it. I was impressed I was up there. I ended finding two others to ride with and rode with them through the McBee climb. 1.99mi, 802ft, 7.6% @ 266watts, 171bpm. I was at my limit for this first climb and close to the limit at the second. Leading up to the McBee there was an epic road descent through some canyons with real smooth tarmac called Weber canyon. At the top of the McBee climb we decide to take a quick bathroom break so we can join up with 4 or 5 riders we can see in the distance so we can ride with a larger group. We waited for 44seconds here after looking at my file.
(Were you pacing smart, feeling strong, surprised by terrain or effort?)
     - I really felt strong and on the first two climbs and was riding solid till about 50 miles in when some rollers had me popped of the back. The first 11 miles were a neutral roll out. The next 16 miles (1hr, I averaged 253 watts (solid)) with avg heart rate of 166. The next 10 miles was avg power of 194watts, avg speed of 20 mph) while riding with a large group that we quite cohesive. This part was pretty chill and nothing to hard. the next sections included some elevation gain and some rollers that were a bit more challenging. I was having to go over threshold on this short little climbs to stay with the group. It started to get a harder and hard and was having to close gaps until eventually I realized it was impossible to stay with them and dropped back solo. Once I did, I let of the gas some since the fatigue had kicked in from all the short burst efforts. I decided to stop in the aid station was some extra time to recharge (2 min 30 seconds) and refilled my bottles. I was using two 26oz bottles with roctane mix and no hydration back for this. 

Settling into the Middle
	3.	During the rolling Horse Heaven Hills section, how did your pacing, nutrition, and mental state evolve?
	   - pacing was fine till I was solo and then it became a bit more challenging. I was still riding at 200-220 watts but it was harder to keep the pace up. I was also getting fatigued from the short climbs and rollers. I was still eating and drinking well, but I was starting to feel the fatigue in my legs. I had a few moments where I thought about stopping or slowing down, but I pushed through and kept going. The scenery was beautiful and that helped keep my spirits up. Lots of wind and was drying to avoid most riders from getting ahead. I think I probably droped maybe 2-3 more spots after being solo which would have put me further back in the field.
(Did aid stations help refill energy? Did you settle into a rhythm or find fluctuations?) 
      - yes at this point, I was pretty dead and just stopping and getting a few minutes to stretch and rest the legs helped a lot. It was a nice reset.

Key Climbs & Moments

	  - The first fifty miles I was punching above my weight and was really happy with the group I was with but it ended up catching up and I had to drop back.

The Finish & Immediate Aftermath
	5.	How did you approach the end of the ride—especially. how did you feel crossing the finish line? I was fatigue and tired and happy to cross. I pushed the last bit to try to get my average speed above 17mph. The last highway section on HWY 397 was brutal with the wind and noise of trucks and cars.

Personal Insights & Reflections
	6.	What went particularly well for you—gear choices, pacing, nutrition, mental mindset?
	   - I felt strong and confident in my gear choices, especially my tire selection for the varied terrain. I used my Santa Cruz Stigmata with 35c S-works mondo tires. This was the first gravel race I had done where I never felt sketchy on the slick (road) tires. It was really quite mellow gravel, hard pack and no large rocks.My pacing strategy worked well for the first half of the race, and I was diligent about nutrition, which helped maintain my energy levels.
	7.	What would you change or improve next time—anything in training, gear, strategy, or preparation?
	   - Not sure, maybe some more specific training for the second half of the race to better handle the fatigue and maintain power.

Broader Thoughts & Takeaways
	8.	Looking back, what lessons from this ride are you carrying forward into future rides or season planning?
	   - That I could hang for 50 miles with some really fast guys. I just need to perfect the endurance to be able to maintain that pace for the full 85 miles.
(Maybe in relation to endurance, course variability, mental stamina?). I set my all time power records for 2h (231 watts ). It was perfect training race for the Sea Otter Fuego XL in April. It push me to the limit and I should see some good fitness gains in the coming months from this effort. My legs were feeling the pain for the next two hours and I even had to take Advil to numb it out. I have never experienced this before so I must have pushed really hard.
