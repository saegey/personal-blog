Pre-Race & Setup
	1.	How did you approach this event — was it an early-season test, an A-race, or more of a training adventure?
	    - I had already done some events earlier in the season and it was more of a training event but still wanted to finish competitively.
	2.	With the reroute making it an out-and-back, did that change your mental approach, pacing, or gear selection?
		- I had never ridden this course before so I didn't really know what to expect. For some reason, I decided to switch to my Lynskey titanium gravel bike from  my Santa Cruz Stigmata. I remember being concerned with mud and tire clearance. I used the 42c specialized pathfinder pros. The bike was did well but I think I had my fit outdated on it since I ended up with some knee pain later in the day that lingered for a few weeks after.  It was really road to gravel climb, to gnarly descent, to road section to Entiat. Turn around road section back into the wind. Gnarly gravel climb (felt steeper this direction and fatigue set in) and then fast gravel descent back to the road.
	3.	What were your bike setup choices (tires, gearing, layers) and how did they play out on the course conditions?
	    - Some of it is mentioned above. I had a 2 x11 Ultegra 11/34 50-34 setup and two 26 oz bottles. There was an aid station in Entiat where I filled up both bottles.  I wore a winter jersey and and I remember it being cold in the start and not ever getting too hot through the day.

Early Race Dynamics
	4.	How did the rollout from Peshastin go — was it fast from the start, or did the group settle in before the first climb?
	    - there was a neutral rollout before the first climb which lasted for about 30 minutes. Things broke up quite quickly on the climb and I was able to stay with lead group for about 20 min of it until I settled into a a good pace. I was happy with my pacing and was able to pass a few people on the climb. I average 257 watts for 56:55 2610 ft gained, avg 4.4% grade for 11.3 miles. I was in good position from the climb. I lost some places on the descent. Others were taking risks but it was loose with lots of large rocks scattered around so consequences of having an issue are high. I hit a snowy patch at the stop and lost the rear traction and narrowly avoid crashing which was a scary bit. I think a few might have crashed in that section since it was so slick.
	5.	Did the change in course profile (compared to prior years) influence how the early groups formed? - 2 big climbs in an out and back format.

Mid-Race Flow
	6.	What was the climbing like — long grinds, steep pitches, or mixed terrain?
	    - The first climb was pretty smooth and not too steep. The second climb was a bit steeper and more technical with loose gravel and some rocky sections. It was a grind but I felt good about my pacing. It was so loose you had to stay seated or would loose traction.
	7.	How did you manage nutrition and hydration — were there aid stations, or did you rely mostly on self-support?
	    - I remember eating some gels and having 4 bottles of roctane. Refilled at the aid station in Entiat. Hydration and nutrtion were good. We had a strong double paceline leaving the aid station with a group of 20 or so. It was pretty cohesive for 30 min or so getting through a rolling flat section into the wind. If you were solo in the section it would have been rough. I had 3 or 4 rides heading toward the aid station on the same road and everyone was pretty productive working together with each other.  I have noticed in other gravel races some rides have difficulty with this and waste a lot of energy not working together. I think it is a skill that needs to be practiced and learned.
	8.	Did you notice any key splits or tactical moves in the mid-section of the race?
		- When we got to the start of the second climb I gave up trying to hold onto the rest of the group and dropped back. the pace was too high and I was already feeling fatigued. I mainly was trying to keep my position and not get passed by too many people. It was a slow grind the higher we got up like 55 rpm cadence grind and the climbing seemed to go on forever. There was someone had a seen a few turns back that I knew and I was determined to not let them get any closer. I ended up finish about 5 min ahead of them and must have gained some good time on the descent after.
Key Moments
	9.	What stood out most about the course — views, technical sections, gravel quality, or unexpected challenges?
	    - It was hard with all the climbing and the varied terrain. The technical sections required focus and skill, especially on the descents.
	10.	Did the out-and-back format create any memorable encounters with returning riders or tricky two-way traffic?
	    - I was able to get an idea of the leaders and other competition with this format. It was interesting to see how the race was unfolding in both directions.

Endgame & Finish
	11.	How did the final stretch back to Peshastin play out — group sprint, solo effort, or survival mode?
	    - I joined up with another rider, Niko Mills, and I pretty much rode his wheel for the last 10 miles or so and let him finish in front of me. I was worked with another rider on the descent and at the bottom but they eventually started cramping.
	12.	Looking back, what are you most proud of in this race, and what would you change if you could redo it?
	    - I did well on the finish but power faded on the final climb. I was happy with my pacing and nutrition. I would have liked to have been able to hold on to the lead group longer on the first climb but I think I did well with my pacing and not burning too many matches early on. I was able to finish strong and not cramp up at all. I ended up with knee pain for a few weeks after but I think that was more related to my bike fit and maybe the grind of the climb. If I improved my descending speed some I probably could pick up another couple min or so.
