## Before the Race

We built this into the season as one of the main goals (alongside High Cascades 100). I knew BWR would be a sufferfest from last year's San Diego edition, so the mindset was respect the course, go steady, and manage the day.

Logistics were a little adventure of their own: we drove from Seattle, ferried to Nanaimo, and stayed in Chemainus. It turned into a family affair—my parents came up, and Scarlett raced the shorter distance. Plenty of friends made the trip too (Vicente and Ira from BWR SD)—we never saw Vicente on course; he was up the road all day. I pre-rode the final big climb the day before, which proved clutch for pacing and for bracing mentally for the gnarly, braking-heavy descent to follow.

**Plan of attack:** ride an endurance pace, keep the lid on early, fuel consistently, and stay smooth through the technical bits. With a long day inevitable, patience mattered more than fireworks.

## Race Narrative

### Start & Early Trails

We didn't push up far enough before the first singletrack and paid for it—pace got bogged down and passing was tough. It wasn't catastrophic, just a reminder that BWR rewards assertive positioning before the narrow stuff.

### Middle Sectors: Pitches, Puncture, and Patience

Around ~80 miles came a savage quarter-mile kicker (~200 ft, ~15% average). I stayed on the bike and ground through while a lot of folks walked. Somewhere in there I noticed a small puncture with a bit of sealant splash—never fully opened up, but it made me a little paranoid for a while.

Aid timing bit me at the first stop: I rolled in at the rear of a group and had to wait. If I'd anticipated and gone to the front spigot first, I'd have moved through much quicker. Little choices like that add up on a nine-hour day.

### Cowichan Valley Trail Train

On the way back along the Cowichan Valley Trail we lit it up—big group, ~18–20 mph for about an hour. Shadows and crossings kept it sketchy, but we stayed clean. Eventually the pace shed riders until it was just my friend and me. There was a strong rider on a mountain bike we were determined to shake—between aero socks, narrower 40 cm bars, and a tidy position, we finally got daylight.

### Final Climb & Rowdy Descent

The late-race climb I'd pre-ridden took ~43 minutes—steady and well paced. Cresting that, the descent was brutally bumpy and unforgiving. I rode the brakes a lot (hands cooked) and just focused on staying upright. A few singletrack bits near the end felt extra hard with the fatigue—pure BWR grit mode.

### The Run-In

I felt **better than last year's BWR** overall, with enough left to push the last efforts. On the final climb my power was still decent, and we managed the closing miles well. My friend dropped me on a late rise and kindly waited at the top—good vibes to the finish.

## Gear & Fuel

**Bike & Kit**

- **Frame/Wheels:** Santa Cruz Stigmata, Roval CLX gravel wheels
- **Drivetrain:** Shimano Di2 12‑speed — 50/34 & 11–34
- **Cockpit/Contact:** Easton EC70 aero bar & seatpost; S-Works MTB shoes
- **Power/Head unit:** Garmin power meter pedals
- **Apparel:** Rapha Pro Team kit, aero socks

**Tires**

- **Tufo Thundero 40c** — fast-rolling, confident on mixed surfaces. I even fielded questions about them mid-race. Had a small puncture that sealed without drama.

**Hydration & Fuel**

- **Hydration:** Osprey pack + two 26 oz bottles
- **On-course fluids:** ~8 bottles GU Roctane, ~2 bottles Skratch
- **Gels:** ~9 GU gels
- **Carb target:** ~**82 g/hr** (kept me steady; would keep this ballpark for future BWRs)

## What Went Well

- **All-day steadiness.** Managed the long day without blowing up; better sensations than previous BWR.
- **Pre-ride intel.** Knowing the last climb/descent saved energy and lowered risk.
- **Tire choice.** Thundero 40s were money—fast, predictable, self-sealed a nick.
- **Group riding gains.** Efficient hour-long train on the trail section netted serious time.
- **Mindset.** Stayed patient through slow singletrack and aid hiccups; kept the day moving.

## Needs Improvement

- **Aid station tactics.** Enter from the front, prioritize fast refill, and get rolling—earns free minutes.
- **Early positioning.** Be more assertive ahead of first singletrack to avoid traffic.
- **Descent management.** Hands/brake fade late—consider pad/rotor check pre-race, hand conditioning, and line choice to carry speed with less braking.
- **Pacing “free speed.”** There were sections I could have pressed a bit more without risking the endgame—especially mid-race tempo.

## Results

**21st** in **Men 35–44** (82 in field) • **89th overall** • **262 finishers**, ~**40 DNFs**  
Emotionally, this felt like a step forward: same BWR brutality, more composure and control. I'm happy with the ride and see clear, actionable places to save minutes next time.
